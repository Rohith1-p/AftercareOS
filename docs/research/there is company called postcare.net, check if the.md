# there is company called postcare.net, check if they are potential compitators

Yes, **PostCare (postcare.net)** is a very direct competitor.

Here’s what they do and how they overlap with your idea:

***

## What PostCare.net Offers

From their site and blog:

- **Automated aftercare follow‑up via WhatsApp** for:
    - Aesthetic clinics, **med spas**, dental clinics, **tattoo / body art**, cosmetic surgery, physiotherapy, etc.[^1]
- **Day‑based sequences**:
    - Messages at **Day 1, Day 3, Day 7…** based on procedure‑specific protocols.[^1]
- **AI assistant in WhatsApp**:
    - Replies to client questions 24/7 in multiple languages.
    - **Concern detection**: flags worrying symptoms so the clinic can intervene.[^1]
- **Protocol system**:
    - 40+ pre‑built templates initially, now **77 ready‑to‑use aftercare protocols** across injectables, skin treatments, laser, body art, dental, cosmetic surgery, etc.[^1][^2]
    - You can upload your own PDF and let AI convert it to a sequence or customize their templates.[^1]
- **Workflow**:

1. Clinic sets up protocols.
2. After each treatment, staff enrolls the patient (takes ~15 seconds).[^2]
3. WhatsApp messages send automatically; AI handles replies and photo analysis; alerts are raised if something looks wrong.[^1][^2]
- **Positioning \& promises**:
    - “Automate your aftercare follow‑up and turn it into 5‑star Google reviews. 2h saved per day.”[^1]
    - Claims: 2 hours saved per practitioner per day, +60% positive reviews, 4.8 average rating.[^1]
    - Strong focus on **review improvement, time‑saving, and fewer complications**.

This is almost exactly the same problem space you’re targeting.

***

## How They Overlap With Your Vision

**Same:**

- Target users: med spas, aesthetics, dental, tattoo/body art.[^1][^3][^4]
- Core problem: paper instructions fail; clients feel abandoned; bad reviews; staff overwhelmed with aftercare questions.[^1][^5]
- Solution style: procedure‑specific **timed aftercare sequences** triggered after the visit.
- AI support: converting protocols + answering patient questions.
- Outcomes they pitch: better reviews, more retention, fewer complications.[^1][^2]

So yes, this is **not just adjacent**—PostCare.net is building almost the same category, just focused on WhatsApp.

***

## Where You Can Still Differentiate

You shouldn’t copy them; you should **pick a sharp angle** they’re not owning:

1. **Channel: SMS instead of WhatsApp (especially for U.S.)**
    - PostCare is heavily optimized for WhatsApp and even argues it’s better than SMS/email.[^5]
    - In the U.S., not all patients are comfortable with WhatsApp; many med‑spa/tattoo clients expect **plain SMS**.
    - You can position as: “No WhatsApp needed. Works with the phone number every client already uses.”
2. **Market segment + pricing**
    - They look more like a **serious clinic tool** (multi‑specialty, 77 protocols, AI photo analysis). That usually means **higher pricing** and more complexity.
    - You can target **solo injectors, small med spas, and tattoo studios** with:
        - Very simple UX
        - Flat, low subscription (e.g., \$79–99/month)
        - No setup fees or long contracts
    - “PostCare for everyone else; we’re the lightweight option for 1–5‑person shops.”
3. **Booking‑system driven vs manual enrollment**
    - PostCare expects staff to enroll each patient manually after each procedure (name/phone/procedure in ~15s).[^2]
    - Your advantage can be **direct integration with Square / Vagaro**, so sequences trigger automatically when an appointment is marked complete—zero extra clicks.
4. **Reputation‑first features**
    - They emphasize reviews broadly, but you can go harder on:
        - **Explicit “Something’s wrong” button** in messages that routes issues to the clinic before they become 1‑star reviews.
        - Built‑in flows to request **Google/Shop reviews** once the client reports they’re happy.
5. **Ultra‑simple AI protocol upload**
    - They allow templates + AI conversion already.[^1]
    - You can make the **authoring UX even lighter**: drag‑and‑drop PDF → immediate suggested SMS journey with tone presets (“clinical”, “friendly”, “tattoo‑shop voice”), so for small operators there’s almost zero setup.
6. **Brand \& niche language**
    - Their brand voice is more clinical and multi‑specialty (dental implants, surgery, physiotherapy).[^1][^6]
    - You can build a **med‑spa / tattoo‑studio native brand**: visuals, examples, wording all tailored to Botox, fillers, lips, brows, tattoos, PMU.

***

## What This Changes for You

- Validation: this strongly confirms the **problem and solution are real and monetizable**.
- Strategy:
    - Don’t aim to be “the only aftercare automation.”
    - Aim to be **“the easiest, SMS‑based, small‑practice‑friendly aftercare automation”**.
- Execution:
    - Study their flows (from public info) to avoid obvious gaps.
    - Focus your MVP where they are weakest: **integrations, price, simplicity, and U.S. SMS‑first clinics**.
