# How to approach tattoo artists for aftercare partnerships

Aim for something very simple: **help them look professional, save time, and make more money**—not “let me test my startup on you.”

Here’s a concrete way to approach tattoo artists for aftercare partnerships.

***

## Who to Target First

- Owners or lead artists at:
    - Small studios (1–5 artists)
    - Custom / appointment‑only shops (care more about reputation)
- In/around Chicago (so you can even visit if it goes well)

***

## How Many To Reach Out To

For the next 2 weeks, aim to **interview 5–8** and be ready to contact **20–30** to get there (cold outreach response rates are low).[^1][^2]

***

## Outreach Channels

Use all three in parallel:

- **Instagram DMs** (most responsive)
- **Email** (from their website or Linktree)
- **In‑person** (if you already go to a shop or convention)

***

## DM / Email Template (Copy‑Paste + Customize)

Short, respectful, non‑salesy:

> Hey [Name],
> I’m a developer in Chicago working on a tool to **automate tattoo aftercare instructions by text**, so clients stop blowing you up with “is this normal?” messages at night.
>
> I’m **not trying to sell you anything right now**. I’m just talking to a few artists to understand how you currently handle aftercare and what’s annoying about it.
>
> Would you be up for a **10–15 minute call** (or quick chat here) sometime this week?
>
> As a thank‑you I can [buy you coffee / send a \$25 gift card / share the results with you first / give you lifetime discount if I build it].
>
> If you’re open to it, what’s a good time/day?

Key points:

- Emphasize **no pitch right now**.
- Show you understand their world (“is this normal?” texts).
- Offer a small, clear **thank‑you**.

***

## What to Ask in the Conversation

Keep it conversational; 10–20 minutes is enough.

1. **Current process**
    - “How do you currently give aftercare instructions?”
    - “Paper sheet, Instagram highlight, texts, something else?”
2. **Pain**
    - “What goes wrong most often with aftercare?”
    - “How often do clients DM/text later with questions? At what times?”
    - “Any stories where aftercare problems led to touch‑ups, refunds, or bad reviews?”
3. **Existing tools / openness**
    - “Have you tried anything to automate this (templated texts, software, Easy Aftercare, Misfit, etc.)?”
    - “What did you like or hate about them?”
4. **Reaction to your idea**
    - “If texts could go out automatically: Day 1, Day 3, Day 7, all customized to your style, would that help you?”
    - “What would it need to do so you’d actually use it?”
    - “If it worked, roughly how much per month would feel reasonable?”
5. **Partnership seed**
    - “If I build a simple version in a couple of months, would you be open to being an **early partner**—using it free for a while in exchange for feedback and maybe a testimonial if you like it?”

Take notes in their own words; those phrases become your future marketing.

***

## How to Frame “Partnership”

For now, “partnership” just means:

- They:
    - Use your early version for free.
    - Give feedback.
    - Let you use anonymous metrics or a quote later.
- You:
    - Set it up for them.
    - Prioritize features they ask for.
    - Lock in a **lifetime discount** once you start charging.

Later you can expand to:

- Revenue share if they refer other studios.
- Co‑branded case studies (“[Shop Name] cut aftercare DMs by 60%”).

***

## Red Flags vs Green Lights

While talking, watch for:

**Green lights:**

- “I’m constantly answering aftercare DMs.”
- “People never read the sheet.”
- “Bad reviews from clients who didn’t follow instructions.”
- “If this just saved me 1–2 hours a week and a couple bad reviews, I’d pay for it.”

**Red flags:**

- “We barely get any aftercare questions.”
- “I like doing DMs, that’s part of my brand.”
- “I’d never let some software text my clients.”

If you hear mostly green lights from 5–8 artists, that’s strong validation to keep building.
